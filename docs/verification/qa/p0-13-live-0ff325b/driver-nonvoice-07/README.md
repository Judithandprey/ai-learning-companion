## One budget for the whole invocation

[`qa_run_live_candidate.mjs`](../../../../../tests/e2e/windows/qa_run_live_candidate.mjs) exports `BUDGET_MS`. The
phases sum to 600 000 ms, and each phase end is measured on a monotonic clock from the wrapper's entry:

| Phase | Ceiling | Ends after |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Preparation: stage and connector checks, looks, watch, payload copy, parse | 60 s | 60 s |
| Native runner, including its own end | 360 s | 420 s |
| Owned cleanup and the watch | 150 s | 570 s |
| Bounded evidence and report | 20 s | 590 s |
| Headroom, used by no phase | 10 s | 600 s |

These are engineering ceilings, not measured durations.

- **Preparation calls share what is left.** Each child call gets `min(its own timeout, what is left of its phase)` and
  is killed with SIGKILL at that end; it never gets a fresh full timeout. This covers:
  - the Linux stage check;
  - each connector check (`sub_copy.mjs`, `qa_live_ledger.mjs`), through a new optional `limit` (their defaults for
    other callers are unchanged);
  - every PowerShell look, signal and parse.

  With under 1 s left, nothing more is started in that phase.
- **The preparation budget is checked at the same four points as the allocation's margin.** Those points are entry,
  before a preflight record, before the output and watch, and before the scratch. A refusal there stops the watch and
  leaves the scratch unused.
- **Right before the launch,** the wrapper checks, in this order:
  1. the watch is still running;
  2. the allocation's wall-clock recheck (`now + native_bound_ms <= end`);
  3. `mono <= end of preparation`.

  Lease checks stay separate from the budget.
- **The native runner is an attached, awaited child of the same process.** `runAttached` uses `spawn`, never
  `spawnSync`, `detached` or `unref`, and short polls, so no timer outlives the child. The 360 s ceiling includes the
  child's end:
  - 348 s after the launch, a runner still there gets SIGTERM, then SIGKILL, each awaited 5 s;
  - after an exit, its output is awaited 2 s (until its pipes close).

  Output not closed in time, or past 4 MiB, makes `output_complete` false and fails `launcher_ok`.
- **What happens on overrun.** The run is aborted and never retried, and it is never a pass. A kill signals only the
  WSL launcher process. The Windows runner's own end is not observed, and the report says so.
- **Cleanup is bounded.**
  - Exact-identity `releaseOwned` runs unchanged: graceful close before force, unrelated processes untouched.
  - Its looks and signals are clamped to the cleanup reserve. Whatever it cannot finish stays `unknown`, never
    `confirmed`.
  - The connector observation and the watch's stop are clamped too.
  - The watch's own end is now part of `connector_released`.
- **Evidence is bounded.** Collection stops before the evidence budget is spent. The records, receipts or checker log
  not read in time each give an explicit error, so `collected` is false.
- **`release_blockers`** lists everything that forbids a release claim:
  - a runner that did not end by itself within its ceiling;
  - owned app or Edge exits not confirmed;
  - the connector not seen released;
  - the watch not seen ending.

  `budget` records each phase's start and the report's time.
- **The command line exits explicitly** once the report is written, so a child not seen exiting cannot hold the
  invocation past its budget. Such a child stays a release blocker.
- **Accounting is conservative** (`qa_live_ledger.mjs`). A run that was launched but has no readable runner results
  counts **every** slot as unknown, never NOT_RUN. A run never launched counts none.

## The runner's work deadline (real source logic)

Candidate 07 passes `-WorkMs 320000`. The runner delta (in `qa_live_candidate.mjs`, appended to the reviewed delta
list; `assertReviewedRunner` still proves r4 plus exactly the delta) adds:
- `[int]$WorkMs = 0`. At 0, the reviewed behaviour is unchanged.
- `Limit-QaWork`: each `waitEval` timeout and each `sleep` gets only the time left since the runner's own start
  (`$qaElapsed`). A shortened wait or sleep is marked `work_deadline_limited`.
- A check before each step, outside the step's own `try`. At the deadline the run aborts there, into the reviewed
  finally, and that step and every later one stay NOT_RUN.

The wrapper reports `work_deadline` (whether it stopped the steps, and which steps it shortened).

`native_steps_sum_ms` (589 s, every step to its own timeout) is informational. The work deadline, not the sum, bounds
the run. The validator now requires `native_bound_ms = 360000` and `native_work_ms = 320000`.

**Residual.** After the deadline, about 28 s normally covers the step in progress and the reviewed finally. A step or
finally that hangs is not cut by the deadline itself: one DevTools read may take 60 s. It is cut at the ceiling. The
runner's results may then be lost, and the ledger counts every slot as unknown.

## Not run, and limits

- **No PowerShell parse of the changed runner.** The wrapper parses the runner before launch, but only after the
  scratch is consumed. A Lead-approved parse-only check of `runner.ps1` `3cf37c29…`, of the bounded `565bc3d` kind, is
  advisable before an allocation.
- **The ceilings are not measured.** 60/360/150/20 s are engineering ceilings. Preparation, run and cleanup times have
  never been measured end to end. A run longer than its ceiling is cut and reported, never extended.
- **WSL interop is unverified.** The effect of SIGTERM and SIGKILL on the Windows `powershell.exe` behind the WSL
  launcher has not been checked. After a kill, the Windows runner's end is not observed (a release blocker).
- **Claude Bash, from the tool metadata only.** The foreground timeout defaults to 120 000 ms, with a maximum of
  600 000 ms. `run_in_background` detaches, and the runtime rules forbid detaching required work. The metadata does not
  say what happens to a command's processes at the timeout. The budget keeps the wrapper's own report before 590 s; the
  Bash call adds Node start-up on top.

  - decision latency;
