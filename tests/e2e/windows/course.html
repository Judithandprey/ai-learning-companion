1. Equation of motion

A mass m on a spring of stiffness k obeys Newton's second law:

m ẍ = −k x  ⇒  ẍ + ω² x = 0,   ω = √(k/m)

The general solution is

x(t) = A cos(ω t + φ)

where the amplitude A and the phase φ come from the initial position and velocity.

Try it: x(0) = 2 cm, v(0) = 0, ω = 3 rad/s. What is x(π/6)?

3. Energy

E = ½ k A² = ½ m v² + ½ k x²

Kinetic and potential energy exchange twice per period; the total stays constant without damping.

5. Damped oscillation

x(t) = A e−γ t cos(ωd t + φ),   ωd = √(ω² − γ²)

Light damping keeps the oscillation; critical damping returns fastest without overshoot.

6. Driven oscillation and resonance

With a periodic force F₀ cos(Ω t) the steady-state amplitude peaks near Ω ≈ ω. The width of the peak grows with damping. Section 6 is here so the page can be scrolled to new content during the check.
